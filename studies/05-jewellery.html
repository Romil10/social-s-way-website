The house
The house

We would rather wait a season than set a stone we are unsure of.

A sapphire earns its place here by how it holds light in the shade of a Jaipur afternoon, long after the showroom lamps are off.

Every stone is examined under daylight, lamplight and candlelight before we agree to buy it. It is then recut if it needs to be, set by one goldsmith from start to finish, and recorded in a ledger that travels with the piece when it is handed down.

i.

Selection

We look at far more stones than we buy, and we keep only those with an even, living colour that does not go dark or grey indoors.

ii.

Cutting

Where a stone was cut for weight rather than light, our cutter reshapes it, accepting a smaller stone for one that returns far more colour to the eye.

iii.

Setting

One goldsmith sees each piece from wax to final polish, raising the claws in 18 karat gold so the stone sits low, safe and open to the light.

iv.

Keeping

Each piece leaves with its ledger page, a lifetime of cleaning and claw checks at the atelier, and a promise that we will reset it for the next generation.

Ring size

Find your size at home, then let us confirm it.

If you are planning a ring as a surprise, or simply want to arrive prepared, a strip of paper and a ruler will give you a close guide to your Indian ring size.

  1. Wrap a thin strip of paper once around the base of the finger, snug but not tight, and mark where it overlaps.
  2. Lay the strip flat and measure from the end to your mark in millimetres. That length is the circumference.
  3. Measure in the evening at room temperature, when fingers are at their fullest, and enter the figure here.
