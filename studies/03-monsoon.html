Our story

A bakehouse named for the rain.

Barish means rain, and the name arrived before the ovens did. Meher Deshpande grew up two lanes away, in a house where the first monsoon shower meant bhajiyas and a pot of chai on the stove.

She trained as a pastry cook, came home, and opened Barish in a former printing press with a tall wood-framed window that fogs up every July. The press is gone, the window stayed, and on wet evenings the whole lane seems to gather in front of it.

Founder and story are sample copy for a fictional brand

01Butter we can name

Our butter comes from a small dairy cooperative in the hills south of the city, churned twice a week. It is the reason our croissants shatter and our nankhatai melt.

02Dough that takes its time

Croissant dough rests for two nights before it is rolled. Sourdough ferments overnight in the cool back room, which in July is the only cool room in Pune.

03A menu that follows the weather

When the bhutta carts appear on the corner, corn goes into our scones. When the rain eases, the chai gets lighter. The counter changes with the season, and so do we.

A day at the oven

From the first loaf to the last lamp.

Knowing when things come out of the oven helps you choose a slot. Croissants are best before noon, and the cardamom knots are still warm at eleven.

  1. 5:30

    The oven is lit

    Sourdough goes in first, while the lane is still dark and the gutters are loud.

  2. 7:30

    Shutters up

    Croissants come out as the doors open, and the first pot of cutting chai is poured.

  3. 11:00

    Second bake

    Cardamom knots and bhutta scones, timed for late breakfasts and early lunches.

  4. 16:00

    Chai hour

    The rain usually peaks now. Bun maska, mawa cakes and the big flask for the office.

  5. 19:30

    Last pickup

    The final pickup slot of the day. Anything unsold is shared with neighbours and the staff table.

  6. 20:00

    Lamps off

    The window goes dark, the starter is fed, and the lane belongs to the frogs.
