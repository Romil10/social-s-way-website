      The room

      A counter, a pass, and fourteen quiet seats

      The dining room was once a printer's workshop, and the long teak counter still follows the line of the old press bed. Ten guests sit facing the pass, close enough to watch each plate composed; the other fourteen sit at tables along the arched windows, where the evening is softer and conversation comes first.

      Counter
      Ten seats facing the kitchen, best for two or for curious solo diners.
      Window
      Seven tables for two, which join for groups of up to six.
      Light
      Low, warm and even, so that the food, rather than the room, is the thing you remember.
      The kitchen

      Cooked from the western coast inward

      Chef Meera Sawant grew up between a Malvani kitchen in Sindhudurg and her grandmother's in Pune. After a decade cooking in Lyon and Singapore she came home to cook the food of the Konkan with the patience of a French brigade: stocks started at dawn, rasam filtered overnight, and every plate built by the same four hands at the pass.

      The menu is written around a small circle of growers and fishermen, most of them within a day's drive of the kitchen.
